Pabbly Connect Pricing Explained (2026): Every Plan, the Hidden Fine Print, and Which One to Pick

Last updated: August 2026 Reading time: about 10 minutes Pricing verified against official pages

Pabbly Connect's pricing looks simple until you actually try to buy it. There are subscriptions and one-time plans, three billing periods with three different discount rates, prices that localize into your currency mid-checkout, and a promo banner that never seems to expire. After helping two readers untangle their invoices last month, I sat down and mapped every plan, every discount mechanic, and every clause in the fine print that changes what you're actually buying.

This is the map. If you want the side-by-side bill comparison against Zapier, that's in what the same 5 automations actually cost; if you're only here about the pay-once deal, the deep dive is the lifetime deal break-even math. This article covers the whole board.

The short version

Most people should start free, then take the Standard subscription at roughly $14-19/month. The one-time plans are excellent value only if your monthly volume is predictable — the cheap $349 tier secretly limits you to 10 workflows and two-step automations. "Unlimited" isn't truly unlimited (fair-use cap around 100,000 tasks). And nothing on the pricing page tells you that your workflow history self-destructs after 15 days.

First, the one rule that makes all these numbers meaningless without context

On Pabbly, only action steps consume tasks. Triggers, filters, routers, formatters, delays, schedulers, iterators, the email parser, the JSON extractor — all free. A six-step workflow with two actions costs 2 tasks per run. So when you read "10,000 tasks," translate it into "roughly 5,000 runs of a typical two-action workflow" before comparing it to any competitor's number.

The traps, spelled out:

  • The $349 tier is a demo in disguise. Ten workflows and two-step automations (one trigger, one action) rules out every interesting use case. It's priced to convert, not to run a business on.
  • No upgrades between lifetime tiers. Buy Standard Lifetime today, outgrow it in a year, and Pabbly's own FAQ says you cannot upgrade to Ultimate Lifetime — you buy again at full price, or move to a recurring plan.
  • No stacking. You can't buy two lifetime plans to double your tasks. The task ceiling you pick is the ceiling you keep, forever.

Not sure which plan fits? Build your busiest workflow on the free plan first — 100 tasks a month is enough to measure your real usage before you commit to anything.

The fine print the pricing page doesn't volunteer

  • "Unlimited" has a meter. Ultimate's fair-use terms describe an initial allocation of about 100,000 tasks that you manually reset, plus a single-business restriction. For a normal small business it behaves as unlimited; for an agency reselling automation across clients, it does not.
  • Your workflow history deletes itself after 15 days. Pabbly's privacy policy states workflow history older than 15 days is automatically removed. Good for privacy, bad for debugging — export anything you'll want to audit later.
  • Support depends on what you bought. Recurring plans get standard email support; the one-time Standard and Ultimate tiers are listed as self-service with no included support, and only Ultimate Plus bundles a year of it. If fast answers matter to you, that clause matters more than the task count.
  • Prices localize. Depending on where you check out, the page may render in rupees or your local currency. The USD numbers above are the reference prices; your checkout total may differ slightly.
  • Security is genuinely solid. SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001:2022, 2FA, AES encryption at rest — one area where the fine print works in your favor.
  • The exit door is real. Every paid plan, lifetime included, carries a 30-day money-back guarantee. Treat your first month as a paid trial.

Which plan should you pick? The decision tree

  • Just evaluating? Free plan. Verify your exact apps connect properly — some "integrations" are webhook setups in disguise.
  • Solo founder / freelancer with steady, moderate volume? Standard subscription, annual or multi-year. This is the sweet spot of the whole catalog.
  • Agency or multi-brand operator? Pro, or Ultimate if you're genuinely burning thousands of tasks daily for one business.
  • Subscription-hater with predictable volume? Ultimate Lifetime ($799) — skip the $349 tier entirely. Run the break-even math in our lifetime deal analysis first; versus Pabbly's own subscription it takes about two years to pay off.
  • Volume that spikes unpredictably? Stay on a subscription. A lifetime plan with a fixed ceiling is a bad home for spiky workloads.

The bottom line

Pabbly's pricing is the best in the category once you understand what you're reading — and genuinely confusing until you do. The free plan answers "does it work with my apps," the Standard subscription answers "what should most people buy," and the Ultimate Lifetime answers "I hate subscriptions and my volume is boring." Everything else on the page is either a trap ($349 lifetime) or a niche (Ultimate Plus).

If Pabbly's rough edges are what's actually holding you back — the dated interface, the day-long support queue — the answer isn't a different Pabbly plan, it's a different tool. Our verdict

Start free, buy Standard annual, and only touch a lifetime plan if your monthly task count barely moves. Read the fair-use and no-upgrade clauses before any one-time payment.
