Pabbly Connect vs Activepieces (2026): Cloud-Cheap vs Self-Hosted Free

Last updated: August 2026 Reading time: about 9 minutes Hands-on tested, no sponsor

Regular readers know the drill by now: when we compare anything to Pabbly Connect, the story is usually "Pabbly's counting math wins, the other tool is prettier." Activepieces breaks that pattern, and that's why this comparison deserves its own article. Activepieces is the MIT-licensed, open-source automation platform that changed its pricing to a per-run credit model — and that one design choice flips the math we've been preaching all month.

Disclosure before the numbers, as always: the Pabbly links in this article are affiliate links, and we earn nothing from Activepieces — open-source project, no affiliate account, zero. When this article says Activepieces wins — and in places it genuinely does — that sentence makes us no money.

The short version

Activepieces charges per run; Pabbly charges per action step. A five-step workflow costs 1 credit on Activepieces but 3-4 tasks on Pabbly — so busy multi-step workflows are cheaper on Activepieces Cloud (~$16/mo yearly), while simple one-action workflows and multi-year budgets favor Pabbly (lifetime deal, 2,000+ apps vs ~400). And if you can run a $5 VPS, Activepieces Community Edition is unlimited and free forever — the only thing in this comparison that beats Pabbly's lifetime deal on pure cost.

The counting math — and this time it cuts against Pabbly

In Pabbly vs Make and Pabbly vs Integrately, Pabbly's trump card was always the same: it counts only action steps while competitors count every module. Activepieces went the opposite direction and made the unit bigger, not smaller:

  • Pabbly: 1 task per action step. Trigger and internal steps (filters, routers, formatters) are free. A "form → format → CRM → Slack" workflow costs 2 tasks per run.
  • Activepieces: 1 credit per whole flow run, however many steps it has. The same workflow costs 1 credit per run. Only AI steps cost extra (2-20 credits depending on the model, 1 if you bring your own API key).

The pattern: the more steps inside your workflows, the better Activepieces looks; the simpler and longer-term your setup, the better Pabbly looks. One honest footnote on Activepieces' cloud free tier: instead of a monthly bucket it drips a small batch of credits per day, and runs stop until tomorrow when you burn through it — fine for testing, frustrating for anything live. Pabbly's 100-tasks-a-month free plan is more usable for a real (tiny) production workload.

Where Activepieces genuinely wins

Self-hosting that's actually free. The Community Edition is MIT-licensed: unlimited runs, unlimited users, unlimited flows, forever, on your own $5 VPS. For a hobbyist or a privacy-sensitive business, that's the best price in automation — full stop. Pabbly is cloud-only; your data always lives on their AWS. (If this route appeals but Activepieces' ~400-app library feels thin, n8n is the other self-hosted answer with a deeper ecosystem — the trade-offs are in our three-way comparison.)

The interface. Activepieces' builder looks and feels like a 2026 product — clean canvas, sensible defaults, no visual clutter. Pabbly's linear step-list looks a decade old because it is; I said so in my review and nothing has changed.

Extensibility. Every Activepieces integration ("piece") is open-source TypeScript. Missing an app? A developer can write the piece in an afternoon and merge it upstream. Pabbly's answer to gaps is webhooks — powerful, but you're on your own.

Where Pabbly quietly wins

The app library, and it's not close: 2,000+ integrations vs roughly 400. For mainstream stacks both are fine; the moment you need a niche CRM, a regional payment gateway or an obscure form tool, Pabbly's odds of a native integration are several times higher. The lifetime deal: pay once, automate forever — Activepieces has no answer to that (the fine print is mapped in our break-even analysis). Zero-ops: Pabbly runs on their servers, full stop — no Docker, no updates, no 2 a.m. "why is the VPS down." And the free plan: 100 real tasks a month beats a daily credit drip for actually running a small business, as we showed in the free plan breakdown.

Support reality check for both: Pabbly is email-only at 24-36 hours; Activepieces self-hosted support is the community forum. If fast human help is a hard requirement, neither is ideal — that's Integrately's home turf (comparison here).

Frequently asked questions

Is Pabbly Connect or Activepieces cheaper in 2026? Depends on workflow shape. Pabbly charges per action step; Activepieces charges per whole run (steps inside are free). Multi-step busy workflows are cheaper on Activepieces (~$16/mo yearly, overage $0.007/credit); simple workflows and multi-year budgets favor Pabbly, and self-hosted Activepieces is free with unlimited runs.

Is Activepieces really free and open source? Yes — the Community Edition is MIT-licensed with unlimited runs, users and flows on your own server. The catch: no AI agents/chat, no API access, no team features, and community-only support. Cloud plans start around $16/mo billed yearly.

How many apps does each support? Pabbly claims 2,000+; Activepieces has roughly 400+ open-source pieces. Both cover the mainstream stack and both answer gaps with webhooks/HTTP; Activepieces pieces can also be written by any TypeScript developer.

Should a non-technical user pick Activepieces? Cloud version, yes — the builder is modern and friendlier than Pabbly's dated UI. Self-hosted, no — Docker, VPS management and updates are real sysadmin work. Use the cloud or Pabbly and pay the small bill.

Does Activepieces have a lifetime deal like Pabbly? No — subscriptions only. Pabbly's one-time plans (from $349) are unique in this class, though read the fine print on tier limits and no-upgrades first.
