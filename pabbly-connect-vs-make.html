Pabbly Connect vs Make (2026): Same 5 Workflows, Two Very Different Bills

Last updated: August 2026 Reading time: about 10 minutes Hands-on tested, no sponsor

Every Pabbly-vs-Make article on the internet boils down to "cheap but ugly vs pretty but pricey," usually written by someone who has run neither. I run both — Pabbly carries my boring high-volume workflows, Make carries the ones that would hurt if they silently died. The short version

Make wins on experience, Pabbly wins on math. Make's visual builder, error handling and debugging are a generation ahead. But Make counts every module as a billable operation while Pabbly counts only action steps — so the same busy workflow costs 2-4x more on Make, and Pabbly's lifetime deal can delete the monthly bill entirely. Simple busy workflows → Pabbly. Complex or business-critical workflows → Make. Most serious users → both, split by workload.

The billing math nobody explains properly

Both tools charge by "units of work," but they count work completely differently. This single difference decides your bill more than any sticker price:

  • Pabbly Connect counts action steps only. The trigger is free. Pabbly's built-in utility steps — filters, routers, delays, formatters, iterators — don't consume tasks. You pay only when your workflow actually does something in another app.
  • Make counts every executed module. The trigger, every filter, every router branch, every tool module — each one is an operation. A scenario is a row of bubbles, and you pay for every bubble that lights up.

Concrete example, my lead-capture workflow: trigger (new form entry) → filter → format the phone number → create CRM contact → post to Slack. That's 2 tasks on Pabbly (two action steps; trigger, filter and formatter are free). On Make the same logic is 5-6 operations every single run.

Where Make genuinely wins (and it's not close)

The canvas. Make shows your workflow as a map — branches spread out visually, data flows along lines you can see. Debugging a complex scenario in Make feels like reading a subway map; doing the same in Pabbly's linear step-list feels like reading a receipt. For anything with real branching, this isn't cosmetic, it's the difference between understanding your own automation and praying.

Error handling. This is the one that costs money. Make catches failed runs, queues them as "incomplete executions," lets you inspect exactly what data arrived, fix it, and replay. Pabbly's approach is closer to "check your history and figure it out." I said it in my review and I'll say it here: a silently dead Pabbly workflow doesn't tap you on the shoulder. For the workflow that sends invoices, that's a business risk, not an annoyance.

Built-in power tools. Make's iterators, aggregators and variable modules handle gnarly data shapes — "for each line item in this order, do X, then roll up the results" — more gracefully than Pabbly's equivalents. Pabbly has routers and iterators too; Make's are simply more pleasant and more predictable under pressure.

Where Pabbly quietly wins

Price at volume — the whole first half of this article. Instant triggers on cheap plans — Pabbly's webhook-based triggers fire in seconds without forcing you up a tier (and webhooks also cover the apps missing from its library). The free plan is real — 100 tasks a month runs genuine multi-step workflows, no card. And simplicity: for a straight-line "when X happens, do Y then Z," Pabbly's list builder is honestly faster to build in than Make's canvas. Not every workflow deserves a subway map.

App counts, for completeness: Pabbly claims 2,000+ integrations, Make around 1,500+. Both cover the mainstream stack; both have gaps in niche verticals, and both answer those gaps with webhooks and HTTP modules. Neither number should decide this for you — check your specific apps on both lists, it takes ten minutes.

Migrating between them: the honest version

There is no importer in either direction — you rebuild by hand. Simple scenarios rebuild in 10-20 minutes each. Complex Make scenarios with deep routing need actual rethinking, because Pabbly's branching model is different, not just uglier. Whichever direction you're moving, run old and new in parallel for two weeks before switching anything off, and migrate your least important workflow first — you'll make your beginner mistakes where they're cheap. Frequently asked questions

Is Pabbly Connect or Make cheaper in 2026? Stickers look similar at entry level, but Make counts every module as an operation while Pabbly counts only action steps — real workflows burn their allowance 2-4x faster on Make. At volume, Pabbly wins on price almost every time.

What's the difference between a Pabbly task and a Make operation? Pabbly bills only action steps (trigger, filters, formatters, routers are free). Make bills every module that executes, including routers and tools. Same six-step workflow: 2 Pabbly tasks, ~6 Make operations.

Is Make better than Pabbly Connect? At three things, clearly: the visual canvas, error handling with replayable incomplete executions, and daily-use polish. Pabbly counters with price at volume, instant webhook triggers on cheap plans, and the lifetime deal.

Can I migrate from Make to Pabbly? Manually, yes — there's no importer. Simple scenarios rebuild in 10-20 minutes; deep branching needs restructuring. Run both in parallel for two weeks before cancelling anything.

Should I use both together? Yes — busy simple workflows on Pabbly, complex or critical ones on Make. It's the setup we run, and it's still cheaper than an all-Zapier stack.
