I Moved My Zapier Automations to Pabbly Connect — What Broke and What Didn't

Last updated: August 2026 Reading time: about 9 minutes Written for beginners

This is not a review. Reviews are written by people who poke a tool for an afternoon and hand it back. This is a migration story: I took the six workflows my side hustle actually depends on, ripped them out of Zapier, and rebuilt them in Pabbly Connect over one long weekend. Four moved cleanly. One fought me for two hours. One broke in a way that took me three days to even notice.

Why move at all? The bill. If you've read my Zapier pricing breakdown you know the short version: Zapier counts every step of every run as a "task," and my busiest month turned a $29 habit into a three-digit one. Pabbly's pitch is the opposite — cheap monthly plans, and lifetime deals that keep coming back like a stray cat. So I tested the pitch with real workloads, not a demo.

The ground rules I set for myself

  • No changing the workflows to flatter Pabbly. Same trigger, same steps, same apps.
  • Both tools ran in parallel for two weeks before I touched a Zapier switch. Parallel running is the whole trick to a painless migration — never cut over blind.
  • If something broke, I logged it instead of rage-fixing it, so I'd have an honest list at the end. This article is that list.

What moved cleanly (4 of 6)

The boring stuff moved beautifully, which makes sense because most business automation is boring stuff:

  • New subscriber → Google Sheet row + notification. Ten minutes to rebuild. Worked first try.
  • New sale → thank-you email + log. Fine, though the email step needed its own connection re-authenticated twice before it stuck.
  • Form submission → folder creation + spreadsheet entry. Identical behavior to the Zapier version.
  • Weekly summary → digest email to myself. The scheduler module is actually nicer than Zapier's. Cron-style control without the cron syntax.

And here's the number that made the whole weekend worth it. Pabbly only counts action steps against your limit. Triggers, filters, routers, delays — all free. My 5-step subscriber workflow costs 5 tasks per run on Zapier and 2 on Pabbly. Same workflow, less than half the meter. Across a month that's the difference between "comfortably inside my plan" and the mid-month "your Zaps have been paused" email I used to get.

What fought me (1 of 6)

One workflow pulls data from an app Pabbly doesn't have a native integration for, so it needed a raw webhook. On Zapier this is a dropdown and a paste. On Pabbly it was two hours of my life: the docs showed a UI that no longer exists, the error messages are fortune-cookie vague, and I eventually solved it by finding a three-year-old YouTube video from a guy with 400 subscribers who had hit the exact same wall. Bless that guy.

This is Pabbly's real tax. You don't pay it in dollars. You pay it in evenings.

What quietly broke (1 of 6)

The email parser. Pabbly gives you a special address; emails you forward there get read and their contents pulled into your workflow. Lovely idea. I set it up, tested it once, watched it work, and moved on with my life. Three days later I realized half the parsed rows had never arrived — the parser had silently skipped messages it didn't like the look of. No error. No log entry waving at me. Just missing data.

I rebuilt that one workflow in a different way (direct integration instead of parsing) and it's been solid since. But it taught me the rule I now follow with Pabbly: after any change, watch the task history for a few days. Zapier taps you on the shoulder when things fail. Pabbly more often fails the way a cat knocks things off a table — silently, while looking away.

The interface deserves its own paragraph

It's dated. Not "quirky" dated — "this reminds me of configuring a router in 2011" dated. Menus where you don't expect them, a builder that works fine once you stop expecting it to feel like Zapier, and small paper-cuts everywhere. None of it stops the workflows from running. All of it is noticeable. If polish matters to you, Make or n8n will feel better in your hands; Make in particular is the smoothest escape from Zapier.

Six months later: would I do it again?

Yes, with one change — I'd have kept a couple of workflows on Zapier's free tier from day one instead of moving everything out of spite. Here's my honest split:

  • Pabbly won the high-volume, simple, run-forever workflows. The bill dropped by more than half and the lifetime deal means it can drop to zero.
  • Zapier's free tier still hosts my two tiny, set-and-forget automations. Moving them would have cost more time than the $0 they already cost.
  • My weirdest workflow — branching logic, retries, an API call mid-chain — eventually left both of them for self-hosted n8n, which handles complexity better than either. Different story, told in the three-way comparison.

Our verdict

Pabbly Connect trades polish for price — if your workflows are simple and your volume is high, the trade is heavily in your favor.

Move to Pabbly if you're a freelancer or solo operator with straightforward, busy workflows and a hatred of monthly bills. Stay put if your automations are complex, mission-critical, or you break out in hives at the sight of a dated admin panel.

If you do move, do it the way I wish I had: run both tools in parallel, watch the task history like a hawk for a week, and keep the tiny stuff on Zapier's free tier. Migration is a weekend project, not a leap of faith.
