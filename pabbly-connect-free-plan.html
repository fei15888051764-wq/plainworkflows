The short version

Pabbly's free plan is 100 tasks a month, no credit card, forever — and only action steps count. Triggers, filters, routers, formatters and delays are all free internal steps, so a five-step workflow burns 1 task per run, not 5. That makes the free plan big enough for 2-4 real, useful automations at side-hustle volume — something neither Zapier's nor Integrately's free tier can say, because both restrict you to single or two-step workflows. It's a test drive you can genuinely run a tiny business on.

The counting rule that makes 100 tasks stretch

Every automation tool charges by "units of work," but they disagree wildly on what a unit is. Pabbly's rule, verified on its official pricing page in August 2026:

  • Free: the trigger (whatever starts the workflow), plus every internal utility step — filters, routers, formatters, delays, iterators, email parser, scheduling.
  • Counted: only action steps — the moments your workflow actually does something in another app (add the spreadsheet row, send the Slack message, create the contact).

Concrete example: "new form response → filter out spam → format the phone number → add row to Google Sheets → notify me in Slack." Five visible steps. On Pabbly that's 2 tasks per run (Sheets + Slack). The same logic on Make is 5 operations per run, and on Zapier you can't even build it on the free plan. I broke down the full counting war in Pabbly vs Make — the short version is that Pabbly's 100 free tasks cover roughly the same real work as 300-500 counted units elsewhere.

What the free plan doesn't tell you upfront

Three honest caveats from actually living on it. First, 100 tasks vanish fast once a workflow gets busy — one viral form day can eat a third of your month. Second, when you hit the cap your workflows simply pause; nothing breaks or deletes, but a paused lead workflow is a silent leak, so set a mental alarm at 80. Third, the free plan inherits Pabbly's platform-wide quirks — the dated interface, email-only support, and the 15-day workflow history wipe I complained about in my one-month review. Free doesn't mean flawless; it means free.
