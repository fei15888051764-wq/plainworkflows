What Is n8n? A Plain-English Guide for Non-Technical Business Owners

Last updated: July 2026 Reading time: about 10 minutes Written for beginners

If you run a small business, you probably repeat the same computer chores every day. Copying a new customer's details from an email into a spreadsheet. Sending the same follow-up message after every sale. Downloading an invoice and re-uploading it somewhere else. None of this is hard — it just eats your time, one small task at a time.

n8n is a tool that does those chores for you, automatically, while you get on with actual work. This guide explains what n8n is, what it can realistically do for a non-technical person, what it costs, and where it falls short. No jargon. Where a technical word is unavoidable, we explain it the moment it appears.

So, what is n8n exactly?

n8n is a workflow automation tool. A "workflow" is just a chain of steps: when this happens, do that, then do this other thing. For example: when a new order arrives in your online shop, add the customer to your mailing list, then send yourself a message with the order details. You draw that chain once, and n8n runs it every time the trigger event happens.

You build workflows on a visual canvas by connecting blocks called nodes. Each node does one job — "read new emails", "add a row to a spreadsheet", "send a Slack message". You drag nodes onto the canvas, connect them with lines, and fill in a few boxes. There is no traditional programming involved for most everyday workflows.

The thing that makes n8n different from better-known tools like Zapier is that it is open source. That means the software's code is public, and — this is the important part for your wallet — you are allowed to run it on your own server completely free of charge, with no limits on how much you use it. More on that in the pricing section below.

What can it actually do for a small business?

Here are the kinds of jobs real small businesses hand over to n8n:

  • Lead handling. A new enquiry comes in through your website form. n8n adds the person to your spreadsheet, sends them a welcome email, and pings you on your phone — all within seconds.
  • Invoicing and admin. When a payment arrives, n8n files the receipt in the right folder and logs it in your bookkeeping sheet.
  • Content and marketing. Publish a blog post once, and n8n shares it to your social channels and your mailing list automatically.
  • AI-powered chores. n8n can pass text to an AI model — for example, to summarize a long customer email, draft a reply, or sort incoming messages by topic — and then act on the result.
  • Reminders and follow-ups. If a client hasn't replied in three days, n8n nudges them (or nudges you) so nothing slips through the cracks.

n8n offers 400+ native integrations — ready-made connections to popular apps like Gmail, Google Sheets, Slack, Shopify, Notion, and hundreds more. And if an app isn't on the list, there's a universal HTTP Request node that can talk to almost any online service that has an API (an API is simply a way for two programs to talk to each other — most modern apps have one). In practice, that means n8n can connect to almost anything.

It's also worth knowing that n8n 2.0, released in January 2026, includes more than 70 AI nodes. These let you plug AI services directly into your workflows — summarizing, classifying, generating text, or building simple AI assistants — without stitching tools together yourself. If "AI automation" is what brought you here, this is where n8n is strongest right now.

Who is n8n for — and who is it not for?

It's a good fit if: you repeat the same computer tasks every week; you want serious automation without a serious monthly bill; you're comfortable learning a new tool the way you once learned a new phone app; and you like the idea of owning your setup rather than renting it forever.

It's probably not for you if: you want something that works in five minutes with zero learning curve and you're happy to pay extra for that convenience. In that case, a tool like Zapier is built for you — we compare all three in our n8n vs Make vs Zapier head-to-head.

Let's be honest about the learning curve: n8n is easier than coding, but harder than Zapier. Expect to spend a weekend getting comfortable. The good news is that the visual editor shows your data moving from step to step, so when something goes wrong you can usually see exactly where.

Self-hosted vs n8n Cloud: the one decision that matters

With n8n you choose between two ways of running it, and the choice changes both the price and the effort involved.

Self-hosted means you install n8n on your own server — typically a cheap rented virtual server (a "VPS", which is just a small computer you rent in a data center) or even a machine in your office. The software itself is completely free, with no usage limits. You only pay for the server, which commonly costs around $15 a month. The catch: you're responsible for keeping it running, updating it, and backing it up. That's not scary, but it is a chore.

n8n Cloud is the company's own hosted version. They run the servers, apply the updates, and handle the boring maintenance. You just log in and build workflows. You pay a monthly subscription for that convenience, with limits on how many times your workflows can run each month (each full run of a workflow is called an "execution").

Simple rule of thumb: if the words "server maintenance" make you tired, pick Cloud. If you'd rather trade a little setup effort for a much smaller bill, self-host.

Pricing, in plain numbers (July 2026)

  • Self-hosted n8n: free software, unlimited workflows and unlimited executions. You pay only for your own server — roughly $15/month for a typical small VPS.
  • n8n Cloud Starter: around €20–24/month, including 2,500 workflow executions per month.
  • n8n Cloud Pro: around €50–60/month, including 10,000 workflow executions per month.

One detail makes n8n's cloud pricing friendlier than it first looks: the limit counts workflow executions, not individual steps. A ten-step workflow that runs once counts as one execution. Competing tools often count every single step — which is why the same busy workflow can cost wildly different amounts on different platforms. Honest pros and cons

Pros

  • Self-hosted version is completely free with no usage limits — unmatched value.
  • Cloud pricing counts whole workflow runs, not every step, so costs stay predictable.
  • Handles complex, branching logic that simpler tools struggle with.
  • 70+ AI nodes (since n8n 2.0) make AI-powered workflows genuinely practical.
  • 400+ native integrations, plus a universal HTTP Request node for everything else.
  • You keep control of your data when you self-host — a real plus for privacy-conscious businesses.

Cons

  • Steeper learning curve than Zapier — budget a weekend to get comfortable.
  • Self-hosting means you own the maintenance: updates, backups, the occasional hiccup.
  • Fewer ready-made app connections than Zapier's enormous library.
  • The interface assumes a little technical curiosity; total beginners may feel lost in the first hour.

Getting started: your first workflow in an afternoon

Here's the path we'd recommend to a complete beginner:

  1. Pick one annoying task. Not five — one. A good first target is "when someone fills in my contact form, add them to a spreadsheet and email me."
  2. Choose your setup. Sign up for an n8n Cloud trial if you want the easy road, or install the free self-hosted version if you're feeling adventurous and want the zero-cost road.
  3. Start from a template. n8n includes a library of ready-made workflow templates for common jobs. Find one close to your task, import it, and swap in your own accounts. This is much easier than starting from a blank canvas.
  4. Test with real data. Run the workflow once, by hand, with one real example. Check that every step did what you expected before turning it on for good.
  5. Turn it on and watch it for a week. Check the run history every few days at first. Once you trust it, stop thinking about it — that's the whole point.

One workflow that works reliably is worth more than ten that sort of work. Start small, get the win, then expand.
